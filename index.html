I am a PhD researcher at Jönköping International Business School, working at the intersection of entrepreneurship, family business, and well-being. My research focuses on the person–venture interface: the relationship between people and the ventures they build and work in. I see well-being and venture organising as mutually constitutive. The way a venture is organised shapes the well-being of the people in it, and people's well-being, in turn, shapes how they organise, run, and persist with their ventures. Rather than treating well-being only as an outcome of work, I study it as part of how entrepreneurial and family business activity unfolds.

My current projects examine family firms as workplaces, focusing on how they shape the well-being of their employees. I also study entrepreneurs managing chronic and life-threatening health conditions, examining how the decoupling of entrepreneur and venture redefines entrepreneurial persistence, shifting it from continuous personal engagement toward sustaining the venture's operational continuity. A related project takes a temporal lens to examine how time is redefined for entrepreneurs managing chronic illness.

I am broadly interested in well-being, family firms as workplaces, entrepreneurial persistence, and organisational behaviour.
